<h1>DAY Day 23 - SENSES: TASTE</h1>
<h2>CONTINENTAL PORTUGAL TASTE ATLAS</h2>
<p>You would be hard-pressed to find a place in Portugal with bad food. From North to South there are some interesting differences in the way the same products are named, used and cooked. I was interested in finding out if placenames somehow tell something about the way food is ingrained in our culture. So, from a total of 20698 placenames located in Continental Portugal, extracted from the OpenStreetMap database, I defined 7 categories related to food, which encompass 1046 of those places. The map on the right shows the way they are distributed throughout the country. The smaller maps show the distribution of each category.</p>
<p>All the categories include several different kinds of the canonical designation. There is a prevalence of placenames related to fruit (363 occurrences) and nuts (317). Meat (140) and vegetables (71) also make a good appearance. Food wouldn't be the same without seasoning (32). Strangely enough for a country with a rich culinary tradition regarding fish, there are only 20 placenames related to it.</p>
<p>The only more or less clear correlation between the location of these placenames and the very nature of the territory, has to do with the fact that the northern part of the country is more populated than the south, with the exception of the Algarve. The same goes to the fact that more people live near the coast than near the eastern border. So, there is an overabundance of placenames towards the northwest and the Algarve. A more detailed analysis concerning the names themselves could provide finer insights into the way agriculture and livestock farming may have influenced place naming.</p>
